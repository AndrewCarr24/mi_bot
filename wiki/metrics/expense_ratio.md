# Expense Ratio (MI-specific)

> The expense ratio measures operating cost as a share of premium — but unlike the loss ratio, the per-company definitions differ — MGIC's glossary uses net premiums written as the denominator while peers use net premiums earned, Arch discloses acquisition and operating components separately rather than one ratio, and NMI does not print a ratio at all (see the per-company definitions below) — so cross-company comparisons require methodology care. At a harmonized level, FY2025 expense ratios ran from roughly 15% (Arch mortgage segment, acquisition + other operating combined) to 27.7% (Radian MI segment), with the cohort middle around 16-22%. Every MI's ratio improved (declined) from 2024 to 2025, reflecting expense discipline and, at several MIs, higher premium denominators (ACGL_10-K_2025-12-31; ESNT_10-K_2025-12-31; MTG_10-K_2025-12-31; ACT_10-K_2025-12-31; RDN_10-K_2025-12-31).

## What it is

**No single cohort-wide definition.** Each MI publishes (or implies) an expense ratio with its own numerator and denominator:

- **MGIC** defines its underwriting expense ratio as "the ratio, expressed as a percentage, of other underwriting and operating expenses, net, and amortization of DAC to net premiums **written**" — note the NPW denominator, unlike peers' net premiums **earned** (MTG_10-K_2025-12-31 glossary). NPW exceeds NPE in a growing book, so MGIC's ratio is mechanically flattered relative to an NPE-based peer figure.
- **Essent** reports an MI-segment expense ratio on net premiums earned (ESNT_10-K_2025-12-31).
- **Enact** reports an expense ratio of operating expenses (net of deferrals plus DAC and intangibles amortization) over net premiums earned (ACT_10-K_2025-12-31).
- **Radian** reports an MI-segment expense ratio on net premiums earned (RDN_10-K_2025-12-31).
- **Arch** does not publish a single mortgage-segment expense ratio; it discloses an *acquisition expense ratio* and an *other operating expense ratio* separately. Summing the two yields a comparable total (e.g., FY2025: 1.1% acquisition + 13.9% other operating = 15.0%) (ACGL_10-K_2025-12-31).
- **NMI Holdings** does not explicitly print the ratio; it is calculable from disclosed underwriting and operating expenses over net premiums earned (e.g., FY2025: $119.9M expenses ÷ $602.2M net premiums earned ≈ 19.9%) (NMIH_10-K_2025-12-31).

## Why it matters

**First, with cohort loss ratios converging in the 0-17% band post-COVID, the expense ratio is the larger driver of combined-ratio differences across MIs.** A 12-point expense-ratio gap (Arch ~15% vs. Radian ~27.7% in FY2025) dwarfs most year-to-year loss-ratio dispersion in benign credit.

**Second, expense leverage is the operating-efficiency story.** Premium revenue is largely locked in by the in-force book, so expense control converts directly to underwriting margin. Several MIs ran explicit cost programs through 2024-2025; MGIC guided 2026 operating expenses to $190-200 million, below its FY2025 $201 million (MTG_TRANSCRIPT_2025-12-31).

**Third, methodology differences are a real comparability trap.** MGIC's NPW denominator and Arch's split ratios are the two most common errors in casual cross-MI comparisons; an analyst quoting "MGIC 21.4% vs. Essent 16.1%" without the denominator caveat overstates the like-for-like gap.

## Current state (as of 2025-12-31)

**FY2025 and FY2024 expense ratios (as reported or derived; see methodology above):**

| MI | FY2025 | FY2024 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arch (mortgage segment) | ~15.0% (1.1% acq + 13.9% other op) | ~17.0% (0.2% + 16.8%) | Sum of disclosed sub-ratios, NPE basis (ACGL_10-K_2025-12-31) |
| Essent (MI segment) | 16.1% | 17.5% | As reported, NPE (ESNT_10-K_2025-12-31) |
| NMI Holdings | ~19.9% | ~21.0% | Calculated: U/W & operating expenses ÷ NPE (NMIH_10-K_2025-12-31) |
| MGIC | 21.4% | 23.0% | As reported, **NPW denominator** (MTG_10-K_2025-12-31) |
| Enact | ~22% | ~23% | As reported, NPE (ACT_10-K_2025-12-31) |
| Radian (MI segment) | 27.7% | 27.9% | As reported, NPE (RDN_10-K_2025-12-31) |

**Pattern**: every MI improved year-over-year; the ranking is stable (Arch leanest, Radian highest-cost). Radian's elevated ratio partly reflects corporate cost allocations ahead of the Inigo-driven reorganization (RDN_10-K_2025-12-31).

## How it has evolved

Cohort expense ratios drifted down through 2024-2025 as MIs cut costs into the low-NIW environment: MGIC 23.0% → 21.4%; Essent 17.5% → 16.1%; Arch ~17.0% → ~15.0%; NMI ~21.0% → ~19.9%; Enact ~23% → ~22%; Radian 27.9% → 27.7% (sources as in the table above). The 2026 setup is mixed: rising NIW (premium growth) supports the denominators, while several MIs guide flat-to-lower absolute expense bases (MTG_TRANSCRIPT_2025-12-31).
