# Capital Return (dividends and buybacks across the MI cohort)

> With loss ratios benign and PMIERs cushions large, capital return is the dominant capital-allocation story across the six MIs — but the six run very different models. MGIC is the high-payout outlier (~$915 million returned in FY2025, a 124% payout ratio, 12% share-count reduction); Essent and NMI return capital primarily via buybacks (NMI pays no dividend at all); Enact mixes a growing quarterly dividend with buybacks and uses special dividends as a "plug"; Radian suspended buybacks after redirecting capital to the $1.67 billion Inigo acquisition; and Arch Capital retains capital for deployment across its three segments rather than paying a common dividend (MTG_TRANSCRIPT_2025-12-31; ESNT_10-K_2025-12-31; NMIH_10-K_2025-12-31; ACT_8-K_2025-02-04; RDN_10-K_2025-12-31; ACGL_10-K_2025-12-31).

## What it is

Capital return = common dividends (regular and special) plus share repurchases, funded at the holding company from operating-subsidiary dividends. The binding constraints are insurance-regulatory (state approval for extraordinary dividends from the writing entity) and PMIERs (the writing entity must hold Available Assets above Minimum Required Assets after any distribution — see [[topics/pmiers]]). The pipeline is: writing subsidiary → holdco dividend (sometimes requiring regulator approval) → holdco buybacks/dividends to shareholders.

## Why it matters

**First, payout capacity is the equity story in this part of the cycle.** With NIW growth modest and reserves releasing, MIs generate more capital than their books require; the rate and reliability of its return drives valuation differences across the cohort.

**Second, the funding pipeline can be constrained company-by-company.** Radian Guaranty's dividends require Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval for at least three years following the December 2025 intercompany note that helped fund Inigo (RDN_10-K_2025-12-31). Enact's capacity is shaped by Genworth's ~81% ownership — capital return is effectively also a Genworth liquidity channel (ACT_10-K_2025-12-31).

**Third, mechanism choice signals management's view of valuation.** Enact management explicitly describes the special dividend as "the plug" to hit return objectives when buybacks are unattractive at prevailing share prices (ACT_TRANSCRIPT_2024-06-30).

**Aggregate pattern**: the pure-play MIs returned on the order of $2+ billion to shareholders in FY2025 (MGIC ~$915M + Radian $576M + Essent $575.6M + NMI $104.2M + Enact's program), while Arch — a diversified company — recycles mortgage-segment capital into its insurance/reinsurance segments instead.

## How it has evolved

**2023**: Enact paid a $113M special cash dividend alongside buybacks (ACT_8-K_2024-02-06). **2024**: Enact returned $354M (ACT_8-K_2025-02-04); Arch's buybacks stayed token-sized (ACGL_10-K_2024-12-31). **2025**: the high-water year — MGIC's 124% payout (returning more than it earned), Radian's $576M front-loaded before pivoting liquidity to Inigo, Essent's $575.6M of buybacks (ESNT_10-K_2025-12-31). **2026 to date**: MGIC added a $750M authorization and upstreamed $400M to the holdco (MTG_TRANSCRIPT_2026-03-31); Enact raised the dividend 14% and guided ~$500M of 2026 returns (ACT_8-K_2026-05-05; ACT_TRANSCRIPT_2026-03-31); Radian's buybacks remain paused while holdco liquidity rebuilds post-close (RDN_TRANSCRIPT_2025-12-31).
