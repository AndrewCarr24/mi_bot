# Regulatory & GSE landscape

## Why this matters to MI analysts

Private mortgage insurance is a creature of the GSE charter. The Charter Acts of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac require credit enhancement on conventional loans above 80% LTV, and PMIERs (the Private Mortgage Insurer Eligibility Requirements) are the operating constitution that determines whether ACGL/UGRIC, ACT, ESNT, MTG, NMIH, and RDN can write that business at all. PMIERs sufficiency ratios drive capital return capacity (dividends from the operating subs to the holdco), and PMIERs Minimum Required Assets sit upstream of every buyback authorization, debt issuance, and reinsurance decision. A 5pp move in the sufficiency cushion is more material to an MI's intrinsic value than a 50bp NIW yield change.

2024-2026 is an unusually dense regulatory window: the Aug 2024 PMIERs Guidance 2024-01 introduced bond-quality haircuts and RMBS/CRE caps phased in 25/50/75/100% across March 2025 - Sept 2026; the March 2025 Guidance 2024-02 sunset the COVID 0.30x non-performing loan multiplier; FHFA under Director Pulte rescinded UDAP authority and terminated SPCPs; PSPA amendments in January 2025 began the choreography for a possible conservatorship exit. State-level RBC, holding-company law (especially North Carolina for ESNT/NMIH and Wisconsin for MTG), and Bermuda/Cayman regulation of ACGL and ESNT parents complete the regulatory perimeter. Analysts who can map cohort-level exposure to each of these levers consistently outperform.

## Capability gaps observed

The cohort retrieval pattern (Q1, Q3, Q5, Q6, Q10, Q13, Q17, Q18, Q20) is the dominant gap. Each requires pulling parallel disclosures across all six MIs (sometimes with multi-year longitudinal slicing in Q6 and Q18), then synthesizing into a side-by-side answer. The current agent's known >60K-token context-trim failure mode and missed-cohort-coverage risk are both implicated; we should expect the agent to silently drop one or two issuers, especially the smaller-disclosure ACT and ACGL UGRIC subsidiaries.

The 8-K Item-code subset (Q17) and the press-release-exhibit-without-Item-header failure mode hit directly. The Aug 21 2024 PMIERs press releases referenced in Q1 and Q13 are precisely the kind of same-day cohort 8-K exhibits where the body text often lacks an Item header — the agent will likely under-retrieve.

The qualitative-cohort questions (Q3, Q7, Q8, Q10, Q15, Q18, Q19) carry synthesis-genericness risk: the agent tends to produce boilerplate "the company faces regulatory risk from PMIERs changes" rather than specific differentiation. Q15 (FHFA Annual Report vs PAR comparison) and Q12 (PMIERs operational performance categories) test deep single-document navigation and should be easier wins if retrieval ranking surfaces the right INDUSTRY_* doc on first pass.
