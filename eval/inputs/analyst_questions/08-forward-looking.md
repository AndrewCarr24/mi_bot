# Forward-looking guidance & strategy

## Why this matters to MI analysts

US private MI is a six-issuer cohort with broadly similar core economics (NIW, persistency, IIF, claim rates, PMIERs cushion), so most of the *differentiation* an analyst writes about lives in forward-looking and strategy disclosures: how each CEO frames the next 12 months on the call, how each board sizes buybacks vs. dividends vs. M&A, and how each franchise positions itself outside core MI (Arch's specialty book, Radian's Inigo / Lloyd's pivot, Essent's title + Lloyd's incubation, Enact's relationship with Genworth as 81% holder, NMI's "pure-play" positioning, MGIC's quota-share-supported buyback machine). Sell-side cohort notes routinely lead with these threads, and buy-side memos use them to justify pair trades.

These signals also move quickly: an 8-K announcing a buyback authorization, a transcript line shifting from "primary means of returning capital" to "balanced approach," or a CEO letter introducing a new ROE target can reset estimates in a way the quantitative tables cannot. An analyst querying the KB needs to pull these statements verbatim from prepared remarks, Q&A, and CEO letters, then synthesize cohort tone — exactly the qualitative-synthesis work the current agent finds hardest.

## Capability gaps observed

The dominant gap on this topic is qualitative cohort synthesis. Q1, Q5, Q13, Q14, Q15, Q17, and Q18 all require pulling six issuers' worth of forward-looking language from transcripts and 10-Ks and producing distinct, verbatim characterizations rather than a generic "MIs prioritize buybacks while managing PMIERs cushion" paragraph. The known failure mode of trim-driven chunk loss above 60K tokens hits hard here: forward-looking commentary lives in long Q&A chunks, and trimming preferentially drops the very issuer-specific quotes the question is asking for. Expect coverage holes on Enact and NMIH in particular, since their transcripts are shorter and rank lower against generic cohort queries.

The second gap is 8-K Item-code retrieval applied to strategic events — Q9 (Arch CEO transition), Q11 (Radian segment divestiture), Q18 (board changes), and Q20 (conference 8-Ks). Item 5.02 and Item 7.01 exhibits frequently lack a clean Item header in the parsed text, so the agent's classifier misses them and the user gets "no relevant 8-K found" even when the filing is in the KB. This is structural and unlikely to fix without filing-type metadata enrichment at ingestion.

The third gap is proxy / NEO compensation (Q10, Q17). DEF 14A filings are typically out of scope of the KB but analysts ask about them constantly, and the agent currently has no convention for saying "this lives in the proxy, which is not indexed — here are the related 10-K Item 11 references" instead of either hallucinating or producing an empty answer. Q2 and Q4 also expose a longitudinal-vs-point-in-time confusion: when a 2025 8-K dominates retrieval, the agent re-queries (the documented self-doubt loop) instead of grounding in the 2024 commentary that sets up the later announcement, which results in answers that read the recent disclosure back instead of telling the analyst what was *signaled in advance*.
