# Reinsurance & risk transfer

## Why this matters to MI analysts

Reinsurance and capital-markets risk transfer are the single largest lever the six US private MIs use to manage required capital under PMIERs, smooth tail-loss volatility, and convert what was historically a "buy-and-hold" credit-tail balance sheet into an "aggregate-manage-distribute" model. For an analyst, the economics of the program — gross written premium ceded, ceding and profit commissions earned back, attachment/detachment points on the excess-of-loss layers, and the resulting reduction in PMIERs Required Assets — drive net premium yield, margin durability through a downturn, and the size of the excess-capital pool available for buybacks and dividends. Within the cohort, the named platforms (Bellemeade Re for Arch, Oaktown Re for NMI, Eagle Re for Radian, Triangle Re for Enact, Radnor Re for Essent, Home Re for MGIC) and their issuance cadences are watched closely because each new bond establishes the marginal cost of capital and refreshes coverage on the most recent NIW vintages.

Beyond the bonds themselves, multi-year quota share treaties (typical 30-40% cession on a forward-flow basis) interact with profit commission mechanics — a high profit commission means most of the ceded premium flows back when losses don't materialize, so the "true" cost of capital relief is small in benign years and negative-carry only in stress. Tender offers and commutations of older Eagle/Bellemeade/Oaktown notes (driven by run-off and shifting reinsurer pricing) directly affect future-period ceded premiums and PMIERs cushion. Counterparty quality (Lloyd's syndicates, Bermuda reinsurers, panel ratings), reinsurance availability cycles, and CRT bond pricing trends round out the analyst's reinsurance dashboard.

## Capability gaps observed

The cohort sweep questions (Q2, Q6, Q10, Q12, Q13, Q18, Q19) are where the current agent is most likely to fall over. The 10-K Reinsurance footnote and Schedule IV exist in every issuer's filing but are presented heterogeneously — Bellemeade, Eagle, Oaktown, Triangle, Radnor, Home Re each have their own naming and tabular layout. Pulling consistent attach/detach/pricing across all six in one query is exactly the >60K-token retrieval blowup the agent is known to hit, and trim drops the back-of-document Schedule IV before the front-of-document MD&A. Q12 in particular is a worst-case combination of cohort sweep, deep tabular detail, and many small numbers that ranking will deprioritize.

The qualitative synthesis questions (Q8, Q13, Q14, Q16, Q18) hit the synthesis-genericness failure mode and the negative-finding self-doubt loop. Profit-commission magnitude, panel concentration, M-Cover participation, and "what did analysts ask" all require pulling apart literal-phrase matches from semantic intent across transcripts and footnotes. Q14 specifically asks for a negative finding (did anyone write down a profit commission) which historically triggers the agent's re-query loops.

The 8-K and tender-offer questions (Q1, Q9, Q20) are the easiest because the press releases are self-contained and indexed cleanly — but Q9's tender-offer question can still trip the Item-code failure mode if the 8-K exhibit lacks an explicit Item header. The longitudinal single-issuer questions (Q4, Q11, Q15) sit in the moderate-difficulty band where the agent generally performs reasonably but can lose the earliest vintages when assembling 8+ years of issuance history.
