# Capital management & PMIERs

## Why this matters to MI analysts

PMIERs is the binding capital regime for the six US private mortgage insurers — it gates a subsidiary's ability to write GSE-eligible business and feeds directly into the holdco's dividend and buyback capacity. Sell-side coverage of MTG, ESNT, NMIH, ACT, RDN and ACGL is dominated by a single recurring narrative: capital generation in excess of PMIERs Minimum Required Assets, the cadence and size of subsidiary-to-holdco distributions, and the resulting pace of buyback authorizations and special dividends. Analysts (Bose George at KBW, Richard Shane at JPM, Terry Ma at Barclays, Mihir Bhatia at BofA, Doug Harter at UBS) press management on this every quarter.

Two structural features make capital analysis nuanced for this cohort: (1) the August 2024 PMIERs Available Asset Standard update (Guidance 2024-01) and the March 2025 follow-on (2024-02) phase in adjustments through Sept 30, 2026, with ratable haircuts at 25%/50%/75%/100% on March 31, 2025 / Sept 30, 2025 / March 31, 2026 / Sept 30, 2026 — every issuer's reported sufficiency ratio now requires a "current basis vs. fully phased-in" reconciliation; and (2) issuers differ in legal structure (ACGL Bermuda holdco with US MI subs AMIC/UGRIC, ESNT Bermuda holdco with Bermuda reinsurer + US Essent Guaranty, MTG Wisconsin-domiciled with Bermuda Home Re ILS shells, RDN/NMIH US-only) — meaning ordinary dividend capacity, statutory accounting, and fungibility of capital differ materially.

## Capability gaps observed

The cohort PMIERs questions (Q1, Q2, Q12, Q15, Q19) are likely to trigger the documented missed-cohort-coverage failure: the agent will retrieve five of six issuers and synthesize as complete. They also produce >60K tokens of context when each issuer's MD&A capital section is pulled, exposing the trim-drops-early-chunks failure. Q12 and Q19 are especially risky because they require precisely-dated 8-K / 1Q25-10-Q chunks that may sit lower in retrieval ranking than the more general 10-K capital narrative.

The 8-K-Item-code failure mode is most acute on Q5 (buyback authorizations, frequently filed under Items 7.01/8.01 with a press-release exhibit that omits the literal Item header). Q15 is a negative-result question — likely no cohort breach of 25:1 — which historically triggers self-doubt re-query loops; the agent should be expected to over-search before concluding "none disclosed."

The synthesis-genericness gap will hit Q7, Q11, Q18, Q20 — qualitative cohort or cross-regime questions where the agent's tendency is to produce a flattened generic framework instead of preserving issuer-specific language (Arch's MI-vs-non-MI competition for capital, Essent's Bermuda overlay tax/capital arbitrage, Radian's "disciplined capital allocation methodology" wording, MGIC's buyback-first orientation). Q8 (debt + covenants cohort table) is the textbook >60K-token cohort table case and should be expected to lose covenant detail to context trim.
