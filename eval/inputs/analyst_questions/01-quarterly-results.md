# Quarterly results & profitability

## Why this matters to MI analysts

For an MI analyst, the quarterly print is the primary signal of where each issuer sits in the operating cycle: how much new business they wrote (NIW) versus where the in-force book is drifting (persistency, runoff, IIF), what the marginal economics on that business look like (NIW rate vs. in-force yield vs. net premium after reinsurance), and whether earnings power is moving up or down on a per-share basis (adjusted operating income, EPS, BVPS, adjusted ROE). Because the cohort is small (six issuers) and the product is largely commoditized, analysts spend most of their time on relative ranking — which MI is winning share at what price, whose loss ratio is benefitting most from cure activity, and which capital return programs are most aggressive given excess capital and PMIERs cushion.

These quarterly outputs feed nearly every downstream decision: forward EPS estimates, BVPS growth trajectory, whether to upgrade/downgrade based on premium-yield direction, comparative valuation (P/B and P/E vs. cohort medians), and whether the market is pricing in a credit normalization that the prints don't yet support. ACGL is a special case because mortgage is one segment inside a P&C-heavy holding company, so analysts need to back out USMI/global mortgage-only economics to compare apples-to-apples with the monolines.

## Capability gaps observed

The hardest questions in this list (Q2, Q4, Q6, Q9, Q13, Q16, Q18) are all six-issuer cohort sweeps that require either (a) a derived metric not directly stated in any single document, or (b) a qualitative cross-issuer synthesis. These tickle two known failure modes simultaneously — the >60K-token context budget overflow and the synthesis-genericness on qualitative cohort questions. The agent will likely retrieve five of six issuers, get the context trimmed mid-synthesis, and either drop an issuer silently or fall back to a generic framework instead of issuer-specific verbatim quotes.

A second cluster (Q9, Q14, Q16) depends specifically on transcript Q&A content, where short analyst exchanges are buried inside long prepared-remarks chunks. Retrieval ranking misses are likely — the agent has the answer in its index but the Q&A bullet gets ranked below denser prepared-remarks vocabulary. Q9 in particular asks for verbatim management characterizations of "rational/disciplined/competitive" pricing language, which is exactly the kind of retrieval-ranking-loses-to-generic-synthesis failure mode the cohort already struggles with.

Q12 and Q20 expose a different gap: GAAP-to-non-GAAP reconciliations live in 8-K press release exhibits whose Item-code headers don't always carry over cleanly into chunked text, and consensus-vs-actual comparisons require sell-side commentary the KB may not fully contain. Q20 specifically risks fabrication if the KB lacks consensus data — the agent should ideally hedge ("transcripts and trade press indicate MTG had a Q4 revenue miss; consensus EPS comparison not directly in KB"), but current behavior tends to either over-claim or self-doubt loop.
